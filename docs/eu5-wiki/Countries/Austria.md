The Heartland of the [Holy Roman Empire](/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire") has endured the long period of the Great Interregnum, and looks to the future with grave concern. In the face of Holy Roman Emperor Ludwig IV. von Wittelsbach's advancing age, the families of von Wittelsbach, de Luxembourg, and von Habsburg once more prepare for competition over the crown. While Imperial authority south of the Alps is deteriorating, German merchants scramble to secure their trade routes into Italy, as the Hanseatic League relish their dominance over the region of Northern Germany.

Due to the diligent efforts of Duke Albrecht II. the Wise von Habsburg and his predecessors, Austria successfully expanded and solidified its rule in Lower Austria, Styria, and Carinthia. With their position on the south-eastern borders of the Holy Roman Empire secured, Austria can now look towards the grand prize the von Hasburg are determined to reclaim: the imperial crown of the Holy Roman Empire.

**Austria** is a South German duchy within the [Holy Roman Empire](/Holy_Roman_Empire "Holy Roman Empire"). In 1337, it is among the larger constituents of the empire and is ruled by the [House of Habsburg](/index.php?title=House_of_Habsburg&action=edit&redlink=1 "House of Habsburg (page does not exist)").

## Starting position

Consisting of most of the south-east corner of the Holy Roman Empire, Austria is bordered by many nations, among them the powerful kingdoms of [![Flag BOH.png](/thumb.php?f=Flag_BOH.png&width=26)](/File%3AFlag_BOH.png) [Bohemia](/Bohemia "Bohemia") and [![Flag HUN.png](/thumb.php?f=Flag_HUN.png&width=26)](/File%3AFlag_HUN.png) [Hungary](/Hungary "Hungary"). It consists of Upper and Lower Austria, which follow the Danube river south of Bohemia, Styria, and several smaller territories scattered around Southern Germany. Originating as a march of the Kingdom of Bavaria in the late 900s, Austria historically consolidated further territories nearby such as Tyrol and what is now Slovenia, later inheriting the crowns of numerous kingdoms and incorporating them into the Austrian realm. Though Austria and the House of Habsburg focused on acquiring territory through strategic royal marriages, Austria's advantageous position allows the player a variety of paths to become one of Europe's foremost great powers.

## Formation

If Austria no longer exists, it can be formed by a country that fulfills the following conditions:

* Primary culture is Danube Bavarian or Southern Bavarian
* Owns Wien
* Owns 60% of:
  + Austria, Styria, and Carinthia areas
  + Traungau and Mühlviertel provinces
  + Sankt Georgen, Linz, and Braunau am Inn locations

## Strategy

Austria begins in a fairly strong position for a member of the Holy Roman Empire, boasting significant military capabilities. The [Nobility](/Nobility "Nobility") has pervasive influence at game start, and although that is normally a bad thing, the effects of being over 25% estate power for the Nobility as well as the *Auxilium et Consilium* privilege affords your levies a significant advantage in combat compared to the units fielded by your neighbours. This places Austria in a favourable position for immediate war in order to gain territory.

In spite of this fairly significant strength, Austria is hamstrung by a weak economy. Austria has extremely poor [Control](/Control "Control") even well into the [Age of Reformation](/Age_of_Reformation "Age of Reformation"). This heavily limits the income they can derive from directly owning territory. Although this is mainly due to features of terrain, Wien's peripheral nature makes it a very poor capital compared to more central locations such as Melk. Further, Austrian locations have very poor [Market Access](/Market_Access "Market Access"), which necessitates breaking away from the Nurnberg Market and creating a local market within Austria itself. Finally, Austria owns a number of silver-producing provinces which can pose issues. An unsuspecting player may choose to develop the [RGOs](/Resource_gathering_operation "Resource gathering operation") of such provinces whilst not minding how little control they have over them. What this does is drive up silver production, increasing inflation, without meaningfully improving the player's [tax base](/Tax_Base "Tax Base"). This can result in a situation that the player may find themselves barely able to mint, further weakening Austria's fragile economy. For the player, strengthening their economy and centralising production around Melk should be their top priority, as it will be nearly impossible to exert any real Control through Austria's mountain provinces until the late game.

Should the player pursue Emperorship of the Holy Roman Empire, it is imperative **not to rival** [![Flag BOH.png](/thumb.php?f=Flag_BOH.png&width=26)](/File%3AFlag_BOH.png) [Bohemia](/Bohemia "Bohemia"). By remaining on friendly terms with the Bohemians, it is possible to prevent them from expanding by using the Emperor's *Demand Unlawful Territory* ability. Should Bohemia conquer any land that isn't rightfully theirs, they will reliably release it as long as they are friendly with the player and have not yet completed integrating the territory. This action costs ducats though, so remember, a strong economy is a necessity of any Austria campaign!
