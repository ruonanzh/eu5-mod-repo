![Castile.png](/thumb.php?f=Flag_CAS_castile_leon.png&width=128)

The Crown of Castile was formed through the union of its two constituent parts, the Kingdom of Castile and the Kingdom of León, when they were reunited after both titles were inherited by King Fernando III the Saint de Borgoña in 1230. A century later, King Alfonso XI the Avenger de Borgoña rules without much opposition, having overcome a troubled start to his reign following the end of a long regency under his grandmother, Maria de Borgoña.

After reining in the nobility of the realm, led by his relative Juan Manuel de Villena, and having stabilized the governance of the country, King Alfonso XI is now turning his attention to the southern border. The [![Flag of Granada](/thumb.php?f=Flag_GRA.png&width=24)](/Granada "Granada") [Nasrid Emirate of Granada](/Granada "Granada") has menaced our country since the emirate became independent a century ago, backed by the powerful [![Flag of Morocco](/thumb.php?f=Flag_MOR.png&width=24)](/Morocco "Morocco") [Marinid Sultanate of Morocco](/Morocco "Morocco"), which has a foothold on Iberia through a petty kingdom centered on Al-Ǧazīraḧ al-Khaḍrā and Ḥiṣn al-Rundaḧ, and held by Crown Prince Abd al-Wahid Abu Malik Marinid, son and heir of Sultan Abu al-Hasan 'Ali ibn Uthman Marinid.

However, these are not the only troubles for King Alfonso XI, as his relationship with his beloved mistress, Leonor de Guzmán, and his children by her have eroded the relationship with the king's actual wife, Consort Maria de Borgonha, daughter of King Alfonso IV the Brave de Borgonha. With the latter living apart from the King, this rift in the royal family now leaves the looming question of just who will succeed him when he passes.

Will King Alfonso XI and his successors be able to deal with these threats to the Crown of Castile, both at home and abroad?

The Crown of Castile occupies the center of the Iberian peninsula. It’s surrounded by [![Flag of Aragon](/thumb.php?f=Flag_ARA.png&width=24)](/Aragon "Aragon") [Aragon](/Aragon "Aragon"), [![Flag of Granada](/thumb.php?f=Flag_GRA.png&width=24)](/Granada "Granada") [Granada](/Granada "Granada"), [![Flag of Navarre](/thumb.php?f=Flag_NAV.png&width=24)](/Navarre "Navarre") [Navarre](/Navarre "Navarre"), and [![Flag of Portugal](/thumb.php?f=Flag_POR.png&width=24)](/Portugal "Portugal") [Portugal](/Portugal "Portugal") at the start of the game. If Castile can survive the Black Death and the madness of its second monarch they have the potential to to unite the peninsula under [![Flag of Spain](/thumb.php?f=Flag_SPA.png&width=24)](/Spain "Spain") [Spain](/Spain "Spain") for the first time since the Visigothic Kingdom at the beginning of the Middle Ages. Once the peninsula is unified Castile can sail beyond the Pillars of Hercules.

## Scripted content

*Main article: [Castilian content](/Castilian_content "Castilian content")*

Castile's unique content is centered around politics, early game military pressure, and long term unification of Iberia. Castile's Iberian Wedding event chain provides Castile with a powerful opportunity to secure a royal marriage (and potentially a personal union with either [![Flag of Aragon](/thumb.php?f=Flag_ARA.png&width=24)](/Aragon "Aragon") [Aragon](/Aragon "Aragon") or [![Flag of Portugal](/thumb.php?f=Flag_POR.png&width=24)](/Portugal "Portugal") [Portugal](/Portugal "Portugal")). Failure to secure a strong succession can lead to rival claims and wars over the throne. Castile's bonuses focus on naval and military power, along with development and colonial bonuses in the early game. Castile's later game bonuses focus on taxation, religion, and goods production.

With Castilian as Castile's primary culture, Castile also has access to [![Traditions](/thumb.php?f=Culture_group.png&width=30)](/File%3ACulture_group.png "Traditions") [Iberian culture group](/Iberian_content "Iberian content") content, which unlocks unique regular regiments, ships, and buildings.

### Starting guide

*Strategy guide · Age I · Community-written · EU5*

| Castile at a Glance | |
| --- | --- |
| Difficulty | **Beginner–Intermediate** |
| Starting region | Iberia |
| Key rivals | Aragon, Granada, Morocco |
| Recommended capital | Sevilla |
| Religion | Catholic or Protestant |
| Playstyle | Colonizer, Conqueror |

#### Capital and geography

Castile has two natural power centers:

* **North:** León and Castilla la Vieja, centered on Valladolid along the Duero River. Stronger in the early game.
* **South:** Sevilla and Granada, centered on Sevilla along the Guadalquivir. Quickly overtakes the north once Granada and Morocco are conquered. Sevilla is one of the most strategic trade nodes in Europe, and the south benefits from better terrain, natural resources, and sea proximity for colonization.

|  |
| --- |
| **Tip:** Move your capital to Sevilla on day one and build all unique capital buildings there. Place a governor in Valladolid to manage the northern pocket. Moving before unpausing costs nothing. |

|  |
| --- |
| **Common mistake — the Toledo trap:** Many players move the capital to Toledo (the historical choice), centered on the Tejo River. This is generally suboptimal. Central Spain has worse terrain, market access, and goods, and your proximity coverage will overlap with one of your local capitals, lowering overall efficiency. |

#### Day one setup

##### Governor placement

Move your capital to Sevilla and build your first local governor in Valladolid. Place the third governor in **Tortosa** to improve proximity in the Mediterranean and Aragon. The fourth should go to **Toledo**, since proximity coverage in central Spain is otherwise weak.

|  |
| --- |
| **Tip:** There is no need to build governors in Morocco or Algeria — Sevilla already provides strong proximity coverage there. |

Use **naval governors** in important overseas holdings. Good locations include Tunis, Naples, London, Constantinople, and the Low Countries.

##### Forts

Delete most starting forts. Recommended to keep: Sevilla, Córdoba, Toledo, Burgos, and Murcia. Prioritizing the south is important — if Muslim armies siege your southern lands, they will begin enslaving your population. Adjust based on your economy.

##### Military

Delete your starting infantry army and recruit cavalry instead. In Age I, manpower comes primarily from your sergeantry, and levies already supply ample infantry.

##### Economy

* Fix any shortages first.
* Invest in construction goods — **glass, wood, and masonry** — to reduce construction costs.
* Develop the **textile** industry (Castile has strong unique bonuses) and **jewelry** (you start with two silver provinces).
* Build **bailiffs** in provinces with the highest economic base.
* Once cash flow stabilizes, diversify into other industries and build markets.

##### Nobility and the Fuero Viejo event

|  |
| --- |
| **Note:** Keep nobility power above 50% in the first 10–20 years to trigger the *Fuero Viejo de Castilla* event. It grants `+0.10 legitimacy` and `+5% noble satisfaction`. This event is only available in the first 100 years. The power cost is high but worth it long-term. |

##### Starting heir

|  |
| --- |
| **Warning:** Pedro is scripted to be bad. He receives several negative modifiers and events that reduce legitimacy. Replace him as soon as possible. |

#### Early diplomacy

* Improve relations with **France**. Reach max relations, profess trust multiple times, then ally them. France is invaluable for fighting coalitions. Also improve relations with the **Pope**.
* Rival **Aragon, Granada, and Morocco** first. Do *not* rival Portugal yet.
* Start building a **spy network in Morocco** and steal their maps toward Mali.
* Boost **military quality to 10** — the threshold for spawning professional armies.

#### Early expansion

##### Navarra and Aquitaine

At your first parliament, obtain two CBs: one on Navarra and one on Aquitaine. Make Navarra a feudatory, then take Bayonne from Aquitaine and give it to Navarra.

|  |
| --- |
| **Tip:** England is typically busy with or recovering from the Hundred Years' War, making this an easy war. Bayonne is Basque culture and provides a strategic port in a sea tile otherwise hard to maximize naval presence in. |

|  |
| --- |
| **Warning:** Do not take more from Aquitaine. Over-expanding here angers France and raises antagonism in Europe, which you want low for later wars. |

##### Granada

At the next parliament, fabricate a CB on Granada. Vassalize them, take Málaga, and release another feudatory there.

##### Morocco

If Portugal was not rivaled, the *Threat of Morocco* event usually fires, providing a good CB along with a work of art and money on victory. If it hasn't fired within 10–15 years, fabricate your own CB.

Morocco typically allies Tunis. Take the war goal (Moroccan provinces in Iberia), then wait for their armies to land — destroy them on arrival. Afterward, land in Morocco and occupy the northern provinces plus Sus in the south (close to your colonization targets). Continue stealing maps from Morocco throughout.

##### Portugal and the Iberian Union

After securing Morocco, rival Portugal. Because you took Navarra early, Aragon will have limited alliance options and will often ally Portugal instead.

|  |
| --- |
| **Note:** Wait for the Portuguese ruler to die. This triggers a Claim Throne CB. Co-belligerent Aragon means you can enforce the claim on both simultaneously. With France as an ally this war is trivial; even alone it is manageable. Note that you now typically place your dynasty on their throne first, delaying the full Iberian Union by one generation. |

Once your initial ruler dies, the **Castilian Civil War** will fire. Side with the legitimate king — siding against risks losing your PU over Aragon and Portugal. The war is easy since France, Portugal, and Aragon will largely win it for you.

### Age II priorities

* Take **admin focus** and rush **Deus Vult** for religious wars — this will likely be your most-used CB for the rest of the game.
* Continue expanding into the **Maghreb**. Create feudatories or vassals with two provinces to limit their cabinet initially. They will convert religion and culture passively.
* Once professional armies unlock (requires quality >10), build **armories** and recruit early. Research tier two levies.
* Expand aggressively in **Sub-Saharan Africa**. Priority targets: **Mali** (gold fields), then ports leading toward Zimbabwe and Akan — all three have large gold deposits.
* *Optional:* Vassalize Byzantium and feed them their cores. Consider engineering a PU over Naples and engaging in Italian politics.

### Age III priorities

* Focus on **colonization** while continuing expansion in Africa, Asia, and Oceania.
* Choose between **diplomatic focus** (stronger colonization) or **admin focus** (`+10 passive global cultural conversion` and better tech).
* Priority colonial targets for gold and silver: **Central Brazil, Mexico, the Andes, Ecuador/South Colombia**.
* The **Moluccas** are also a strong early target due to the cloves modifier.

### Religion choice

Both Catholic and Protestant are viable for Castile.

* **Protestant** is generally stronger economically. Catholic nations cannot upgrade to **empire rank**, missing one cabinet position and one extra governor slot. Protestant also offers stronger economic bonuses overall. Downside: converting subjects is more tedious.
* **Catholic** provides better diplomatic tools, the ability to reform and improve the religion, access to **Jesuit colleges**, and stronger personal union opportunities.

### Colonial nation management

**Small Colonial Nations** are recommended. Split them once they start accumulating red proximity costs. While they require active micromanagement — and frustratingly do not build governors on their own — they are significantly more economically efficient and tend to develop better than large CNs.

|  |
| --- |
| **Warning:** The downside of small CNs is increased diplomatic capacity usage and more diplo power spent improving relations. |

Give gold provinces to your CNs, but make sure their **capital is located close to the precious metal deposits** — that is where the majority of their income originates.

|  |
| --- |
| **Tip:** Once technology progresses and CNs get better roads, begin merging smaller ones together to reduce diplomatic overhead. This is also a fairly historical approach. |
