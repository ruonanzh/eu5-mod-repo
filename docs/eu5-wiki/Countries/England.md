![England.png](/thumb.php?f=Flag_ENG_monarchy.png&width=128)

The English crown is still reeling from the treacherous rebellion that occurred ten years prior and resulted in the underhanded assassination of King Edward II. Although the ringleaders were executed, several co-conspirators have been granted mercy in the name of peace — a peace which could easily be shattered at any moment.

Meanwhile, King Edward II's surviving heir, King Edward III, has already proven to be a capable young ruler, and is making bold designs on the French throne — a throne which is his birthright as the grandson of a Capet ruler.

In the north, the misguided [![Flag of Balliol](/thumb.php?f=Flag_SBL.png&width=24)](/Balliol "Balliol") [Balliol](/Balliol "Balliol") weakly contends for the Scots crown, while our loyal vassals maintain a tight grip on the governments of [![Flag of Wales](/thumb.php?f=Flag_WLS.png&width=24)](/Wales "Wales") [Wales](/Wales "Wales"), [![Flag of Pale](/thumb.php?f=Flag_PLE.png&width=24)](/Pale "Pale") [The Pale](/index.php?title=Pale&action=edit&redlink=1 "Pale (page does not exist)"), and [![Flag of Aquitaine](/thumb.php?f=Flag_AQN.png&width=24)](/Aquitaine "Aquitaine") [Aquitaine](/Aquitaine "Aquitaine").

How shall we brave the turbulent years to come? Will we cross the channel and become a continental power, or should we look to the seas for global dominion?

**England** is a nation primarily located in the Britain [![Region.png](/thumb.php?f=Region.png&width=24)](/Region "Region") [region](/Region "Region") of the Western Europe [![Sub continent.png](/thumb.php?f=Sub_continent.png&width=24)](/Sub-continent "Sub-continent") [sub-continent](/Sub-continent "Sub-continent"), with some minor holdings in the Picardy and Normandy [![Area.png](/thumb.php?f=Area.png&width=24)](/File%3AArea.png) [areas](/Area "Area") of the France region. In addition to this, England has several [vassals](/Subjects "Subjects"), namely [![Flag of Aquitaine](/thumb.php?f=Flag_AQN.png&width=24)](/Aquitaine "Aquitaine") [Aquitaine](/Aquitaine "Aquitaine") in Southern France, [![Flag of Wales](/thumb.php?f=Flag_WLS.png&width=24)](/Wales "Wales") [Wales](/Wales "Wales"), the [![Flag of Lancaster](/thumb.php?f=Flag_LAN.png&width=24)](/Lancaster "Lancaster") [Earldom of Lancaster](/index.php?title=Lancaster&action=edit&redlink=1 "Lancaster (page does not exist)"), the [![Flag of Durham](/thumb.php?f=Flag_DHM.png&width=24)](/Durham "Durham") [Bishopric of Durham](/index.php?title=Durham&action=edit&redlink=1 "Durham (page does not exist)"), and [![Flag of Pale](/thumb.php?f=Flag_PLE.png&width=24)](/Pale "Pale") [The Pale](/index.php?title=Pale&action=edit&redlink=1 "Pale (page does not exist)") in Ireland. Shortly after the game begins, an event will fire for England that starts the [Hundred Years' War](/Hundred_Years_War "Hundred Years War") situation, which revolves around England and France fighting for dominance over the French throne. England is also involved in the struggle for Irish dominance via their subject of the Pale, which starts the game as the leader of the [![IO lordship of ireland.png](/thumb.php?f=IO_lordship_of_ireland.png&width=24)](/Lordship_of_Ireland "Lordship of Ireland") [Lordship of Ireland](/Lordship_of_Ireland "Lordship of Ireland") and must fight against its rivals in the [High Kingship of Ireland](/High_Kingship_of_Ireland "High Kingship of Ireland").

Moving beyond England's borders and subjects, they start the game off as [![Rivals.png](/thumb.php?f=Rivals.png&width=24)](/Rivals "Rivals") [rivals](/index.php?title=Rivals&action=edit&redlink=1 "Rivals (page does not exist)") to both [![Flag of France](/thumb.php?f=Flag_FRA.png&width=24)](/France "France") [France](/France "France") and [![Flag of Scotland](/thumb.php?f=Flag_SCO_monarchy.png&width=24)](/Scotland "Scotland") [Scotland](/Scotland "Scotland") while guaranteeing the independence of the Scottish breakaway state of [![Flag of Balliol](/thumb.php?f=Flag_SBL.png&width=24)](/Balliol "Balliol") [Balliol](/Balliol "Balliol"), whose ruler they are supporting in the Scottish Civil War. They also begin the game with dynastic ties to [![Flag of France](/thumb.php?f=Flag_FRA.png&width=24)](/France "France") [France](/France "France"), [![Flag of Lancaster](/thumb.php?f=Flag_LAN.png&width=24)](/Lancaster "Lancaster") [Lancaster](/index.php?title=Lancaster&action=edit&redlink=1 "Lancaster (page does not exist)"), and [![Flag of Arundel](/thumb.php?f=Flag_AUD.png&width=24)](/Arundel "Arundel") [Arundel](/index.php?title=Arundel&action=edit&redlink=1 "Arundel (page does not exist)").

## Events and Flavor

*Main article: [English content](/English_content "English content")*

As one of the countries designated as a Tier I nation prior to the release of the game, England has a large amount of flavor content, with 233 [Dynamic Historical Events](/index.php?title=Dynamic_Historical_Event&action=edit&redlink=1 "Dynamic Historical Event (page does not exist)"), 18 unique [Advances](/Advances "Advances"), 8 unique [Government Reforms](/English_content#Unique_Government_Reforms "English content"), 3 unique [Buildings](/English_content#Unique_buildings "English content"), and 2 unique [Estate Privileges](/English_content#Unique_estate_privileges "English content"). England's unique content guide the player through key themes like the Hundred Years' War, internal political reforms, and the rise of institutions like Parliament and the Royal Navy. Mechanically, England's unique content pushes the player towards naval dominance, trade, and gradual state modernization. Its advances and reforms emphasize things like stronger fleets, improved administration, and elite infantry.

## Strategy

![Strategy](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/5/54/Information.png/36px-Information.png "Strategy") *The below is **one of many** player suggested strategies for **England**. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.*

As England, your first goal in a new campaign is to deal with the Hundred Years' War, whether that be by claiming the French throne or by choosing to ignore the continent altogether and instead focusing on consolidating the British Isles. No matter which path you choose, a good first move is to release your Picard holdings as a vassal, and then grant scutage to both Picardy and Aquitaine. This will ensure that in the inevitable wars with France, you won't lose any warscore from your mainland vassals being occupied. In general, as long as you pay attention to where France and France's vassals fleets are, they will never be able to land troops on Great Britain. You have naval superiority from the start of the game, and repeatedly destroying the French fleets and any small stacks they manage to land is an excellent way to farm warscore in the first few wars.

England starts with enough levies to reasonably beat those of France and their numerous vassals, but swarming is still a considerable threat. Regardless of your own actions, France is very likely to declare war on you early into the game. For security, players might want to use their significant starting amount of ducats to built up their navy. Otherwise, continuous fighting with French vassal's navies will cause noticeable damage to your own over time. As your holdings in Picardy are quite small - only two locations - the ideal fighting zone for your first war will be Aquitaine. If you have spare ducats after your naval buildup or are willing to take some loans, building a few castles on Aquitaine's borders can prevent France from picking off your troops during transport. Once you have enough troops on the mainland, you'll want to ensure you only take battles in which France is taking considerably more casualties than you. You can make sure of this by only taking battles where they gain terrain penalties (such as when crossing a river) and/or when they're besieging your castles (in which case, the besieger is always considered the attacker). If you find an opening and have the troops, taking Paris will usually give you a ticking war score depending on the casus belli, though war score from battles will eventually offset their own ticking war score if you choose to ignore the French capital.

Your priority in this first war is to weaken France, so its ok if you can't take much land. In subsequent wars (there will be quite a few, if you hope to get any real benefit out of owning land in France) you'll want to take as much land from France as you can. While the prospect of stealing vassals like Normandy and Brittany or snaking to Paris and immediately forcing a personal union may be tempting, it can create a number of issues. France's larger vassals are big enough that having too many of them may force you to spend more in diplomatic costs keeping them loyal than they pay you. Additionally, France is so large even without their vassals that annexing them diplomatically will usually be a centuries-long process riddled with attempts by the French to break free or steal senior partner status from you. With proper maritime control and infrastructure, you can expand control into France's coastal regions enough to make a good profit off the land while out-scaling revolts. Because there's very little stopping any given nation on the map from expanding, its almost certain during your conquest of France that they'll expand a good amount, or later on that another nation will exploit France's weakness to expand at their expense. How much land you want to take from the French before enforcing the personal union is up to the player, but keep in mind that to do so you need to own Paris and occupy whatever their new capital is. If you want an easier time, you could annex the provinces surrounding Paris and destroy the fort in one of your peace deals, making an easy siege and free 25% war score that the French will be less and less able to defend.

If you prefer give up your continental holdings and ignore the Hundred Years' War, all you need to do is maintain a decently strong navy (at least 20 as-modern-as-possible galleys is recommended, ideally 30). France will declare war on you multiple times via situation-triggered events, so make sure your navy is able to defend the home islands whenever they do. Eventually, once France annexes enough of their vassals, the Hundred Years' War situation will end.
