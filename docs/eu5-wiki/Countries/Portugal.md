The Kingdom of Portugal won its independence after a long and hard-fought struggle against both the Muslim and Christian rulers of the Iberia Region. After enacting the important social, economic, and cultural reforms promoted by King Dinis, his son and successor, King Alfonso IV the Brave de Borgonha may now face different challenges and opportunities while ruling over the Portuguese people.

To the west and south lies the wide North Atlantic Ocean and the promise of new trade routes with foreign lands, while to the east the powerful [![Flag of Castile](/thumb.php?f=Flag_CAS_castile_leon.png&width=24)](/Castile "Castile") [Crown of Castile](/Castile "Castile"), with strong dynastic connections to Portugal, could one day lead to a united Iberia under Portuguese leadership.

Whatever the future holds, Portugal will need to secure its position one way or another to ensure its future as an independent country.

**Portugal** is a country in western Iberia, located West of Castile.

## Historical context

### Government

#### Parliament

Portugal's **Cortes Gerais** ([Parliament](/Parliament "Parliament")) can be called every 60 months.

### Diplomacy

Portugal starts with no alliance, a decaying good relations bonus with Castille (to avoid getting eaten up right at the start), and the possibility to join Tlemcen in the war against Morocco.

## Scripted content

*Main article: [Portuguese content](/Portuguese_content "Portuguese content")*

Portugal's content is focused on early exploration and maritime expansion as a path to power. Portugal benefits from early access to exploration tools and systems tied to colonization, allowing it to reach key islands, Africa, and the New World before other colonial powers. Portugal begins the game economically weak and underpopulated, but its unique content reward building maritime and trade infrastructure, and extracting wealth from colonies and trade routes. Portugal's scripted events revolve around politics within the Iberian peninsula and trade opportunities overseas. Starting with the Portuguese primary culture, Portugal also has access to [![Traditions](/thumb.php?f=Culture_group.png&width=30)](/File%3ACulture_group.png "Traditions") [Iberian bonuses](/Iberian_content "Iberian content"), which unlock unique regular regiments, ships, and buildings that provide additional military and naval power, along with additional research and proximity bonuses.

### Unique Advances

Portugal has access to 13 unique advances. Early game advances focus mostly on naval power and colonization, as well as infantry power and goods production. Later game advances focus on military power, crown power, and subject loyalty.

### Unique government reforms

Portugal has has access to 4 unique government reforms, Portuguese Monarchy (naval and taxation bonuses), Manueline Architecture (cultural tradition and building cost bonuses), Casa da Índia (subject and trade bonuses), and Portuguese Ordinances (legislative and tax bonuses),. More details can be found in [Portugal's content page](/Portuguese_content#Unique_government_reforms "Portuguese content").

### Unique policies

Portugal has has access to 3 unique policies that provide colonial and trade bonuses.

### Unique buildings

Portugal has has access to the Feitorias building, which provides powerful local trade bonuses.

## Strategy

### Opening Moves

At game start, Portugal is saddled with far more forts than it needs. While destroying even a few will bring the country below the maximum and reduce the extra penalties, there's little stopping Portugal from removing nearly all of them and enjoying the resulting influx of funds. The saved money can be better spent on scriptoriums, libraries, hospitals, and high-profit RGOs.

Scripted content associated with rulers' deaths will offer opportunities to significantly boost legitimacy and stability; while it is difficult to over-invest in stability, there is less of an urgency to raise legitimacy above 80.

#### Itinerant Court

Portugal starts with the Itinerant Court court custom policy, which means that it will be made to move its capital (for free) each time a ruler dies (or pay a 5 legitimacy penalty). Portugal's ample coastline makes this policy much more viable, and it is generally possible to simply bounce back and forth between Porto and Lisboa as the capital. However, if new territory is conquered, the policy is disproportionately likely to limit new capital sites to the recently acquired territories, which could be rather harmful to Portugal's control if land is seized that isn't contiguous with the Portuguese homelands.
