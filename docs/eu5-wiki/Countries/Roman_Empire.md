The **Roman Empire** is a [formable nation](/Formable_country "Formable country"), not existing at the start of the game but able to emerge from countries that fulfill certain criteria.

## Formation

### Formation

:   *Not since the second century has one empire commanded the entirety of the Mediterranean and beyond, and that empire was mighty Rome. If we claim this land, we can scoff at whatever pretenders may claim in Constantinople or the Holy Roman Empire, and proclaim ourselves as the* true *successors of the Roman Empire.* Cives Romani sumus*!*

#### Conditions

* Game rule for formable countries set to "Allow Ahistorical"
* Is not [![Flag of Byzantium](/thumb.php?f=Flag_BYZ.png&width=24)](/Byzantium "Byzantium") [Byzantium](/Byzantium "Byzantium"), [![Flag of Trebizond](/thumb.php?f=Flag_TRE.png&width=24)](/Trebizond "Trebizond") [Trebizond](/Trebizond "Trebizond"), or [![Flag of Papal States](/thumb.php?f=Flag_PAP.png&width=24)](/Papal_States "Papal States") [Papal States](/Papal_States "Papal States")
* [![Flag of Byzantium](/thumb.php?f=Flag_BYZ.png&width=24)](/Byzantium "Byzantium") [Byzantium](/Byzantium "Byzantium") does not exist
* Is a Hegemon and has the Empire country rank
* State religion is either [![Religion hellenism religion.png](/thumb.php?f=Religion_hellenism_religion.png&width=24)](/File%3AReligion_hellenism_religion.png) [Hellenic](/List_of_religions#European_Folk_religions "List of religions") or in the [Christianity](/List_of_religions#Christian_religions "List of religions") religion group
* Owns locations Rome (3068) and Constantinople (5877)
* Owns 75% of locations within the following regions:
  + Italy
  + Iberia
  + France
  + Balkans
  + Britain
  + Anatolia
  + Southern Germany
  + Crescent
  + Egypt
  + Maghreb
  + Carpathia
  + Caucasus

#### Effects

* Sets court language to Latin
* Sets capital to Rome
* If [![DLC Fate of the Phoenix.png](/thumb.php?f=DLC_Fate_of_the_Phoenix.png&width=24)](/File%3ADLC_Fate_of_the_Phoenix.png) [Fate of the Phoenix](/Fate_of_the_Phoenix "Fate of the Phoenix") is enabled:
  + Unlocks Restore Roman Borders casus belli

### Formation (Byzantium)

:   *We are the true successors of the Roman Empire, and we will not allow barbarians to claim our legacy! By reestablishing ourselves in Italia, we will prove to the rump states of the West that we are the true heirs of Rome!*

#### Conditions

* Is [![Flag of Byzantium](/thumb.php?f=Flag_BYZ.png&width=24)](/Byzantium "Byzantium") [Byzantium](/Byzantium "Byzantium"), or is [![Flag of Trebizond](/thumb.php?f=Flag_TRE.png&width=24)](/Trebizond "Trebizond") [Trebizond](/Trebizond "Trebizond") and [![Flag of Byzantium](/thumb.php?f=Flag_BYZ.png&width=24)](/Byzantium "Byzantium") [Byzantium](/Byzantium "Byzantium") does not exist
* Has the Empire country rank
* Owns locations Rome (3068) and Constantinople (5877)
* Owns 25% of locations in the Italy region

#### Effects

* Sets capital to Rome
* Every non-integrated location in Marittima province becomes integrated
* Moves +33 towards Latinitas
* If [![DLC Fate of the Phoenix.png](/thumb.php?f=DLC_Fate_of_the_Phoenix.png&width=24)](/File%3ADLC_Fate_of_the_Phoenix.png) [Fate of the Phoenix](/Fate_of_the_Phoenix "Fate of the Phoenix") is enabled:
  + Unlocks Restore Roman Borders casus belli

## Strategy

Considering the sheer amount of territory required to form the Roman Empire, it'll be a long and arduous campaign no matter what you do or how skilled you are at the game. Nevertheless, there are several ways to make forming the Roman Empire much easier.

Most important is what country you begin as. While some might look to Byzantium for its strong historical claim to being a direct continuation of Rome or France for its very strong starting position, both of these nations will face potentially avoidable obstacles during a campaign. In Byzantium's case, they will be forced to spend at least the first few decades of the campaign struggling through extreme internal strife, diplomatic isolation, a debilitated economy, and a legion of ravenous neighbors (including the Ottomans, possibly the strongest country in the game if not for their small starting size). While France starts both powerful and large, their loyalty of their horde of subjects is never certain. To make things worse for the French, they're immediately surrounded by powerful rivals like Castile and England (the latter of which is France's main opponent in the Hundred Years' War, a situation beginning a month after the start of the game), and any quick expansion to the east risks provoking a coalition of hundreds of German states. Considering all this, you may want to select a nation with a good balance of starting size and starting position. For example, Aragon is both capable of defending itself against its stronger neighbors and is well-positioned to expand into Italy and North Africa, with additional options of Western Iberia and France should they eclipse the stronger powers of the region. Naples also has these same benefits at the start of the game, with additional opportunities to expand into Greece, Anatolia, and the Levant. Both nations' long coast and seaside capitals also make projecting control across their growing empires easier. While any nation can be used, choosing nations like these may make the job much easier.

No matter what nation you chose, there are certain necessities for a successful (and less irritating) Roman restoration campaign. Be sure not to overstretch yourself, as overexpansion will put severe and difficult-to-reverse economic strain on your nation, while making defense and suppression of rebels very difficult. Consider forming subjects along cultural lines to reduce the number of discriminated cultures in your nation without exceeding your cultural capacity, though be cautious of their loyalty, which is harder to maintain the more subjects you have and the larger they are. After you've expanded significantly, consider taking a break to build infrastructure that will prepare your economy for future wars and expand the level of control you have over your empire. A Naples that controls the whole Mediterranean coast but has no control outside of Southern Italy is barely stronger than it was at the beginning of the game. Antagonism will inevitably build up as you conquer vast amounts of land, so consider splitting your conquests between different frontiers. If Aragon has almost accrued enough antagonism for Castile, Portugal, and France to join a coalition against it, it may be ideal to avoid expanding in that same direction until that antagonism has decayed and instead expand in another direction.
