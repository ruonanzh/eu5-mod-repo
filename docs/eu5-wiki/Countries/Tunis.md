## Strategy

![Strategy](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/thumb/5/54/Information.png/36px-Information.png "Strategy") *The below is **one of many** player suggested strategies for **Tunis**. Bear in mind, due to the dynamic nature of the game, it may unfold differently for other players.*

Boasting considerable military capabilities, many subjects, a strong capital location and a lot of land; Tunis materially begins the game in a strong position. However, in spite of these strengths, Tunis is hamstrung by political weaknesses which are likely to prevent the player from taking much action until internal affairs are put in order.

Tunis' proximity to northern Mediterranean markets ultimately means that the nation will strongly benefit from a robust focus on trade. However, Tunis' political instability will prevent the player from reaping any trade rewards for a considerable amount of time. Extremely low Crown Power will destroy trade profitability, and therefore, player income. In addition, Tunis' early events will cripple both stability and legitimacy. Although instinct may be to quickly revoke privileges to raise Crown Power as well as spend generously to restore stability and legitimacy, doing these things is liable to inflict much more harm than good. Tunis has the fortune of beginning the game in an extremely safe position; unless the player chooses to antagonise Morocco or Egypt deliberately, the player has as much time as they'd like to safely set their affairs in order. With patience, and willingness to slowly make reforms over the period of a whole century, Tunis is likely to emerge from their instability as one of the more dominant forces in the Mediterranean.

It is worth noting that your ally, Morocco, is almost guaranteed to call you in to their war with Tlemcen at the game start. If prepared for this, it is possible for the player to quickly occupy Tlemcen's territories and sign a separate peace treaty with them. This enables the player to conquer some provinces or set up a fiefdom on their western border very early on.
