|  |  |

*Attempts at enforcing the religious peace between Hussite and Catholic followers have broken down despite our best efforts. Jiří z Poděbrad, leader of the Hussite faction, has raised an army and now marches upon Praha. He means to make himself King of Bohemia and secure religious freedom for the followers of Jan Hus.*

---

*Historical info:*

*George of Kunštát and Poděbrady was a Bohemian noble and leader of the Hussites. Due to tensions between those faithful to Catholicism and Hussites during the reign of King Ladislaus, he gathered an army and marched to the capital and afterwards defeated all nobility still loyal to the Pope. In 1451, he was granted the administration of Bohemia by the Holy Roman Emperor Frederik III, and subsequently in 1458 he was elected by the estates of Bohemia, even by those still loyal to the Pope. His reign was marked by policies of peace and cooperation, but the Catholic opposition organized a rebellion with the help of King Matthias Corvinus of Hungary, who invaded Bohemia to restore it to Catholicism.*

---

This event happens *only once* during a campaign.

### The Vladislav Hall

*Benedikt Rejt, our wonderful architect, has truly outdone himself with his latest construction, the Vladislav Hall. An absolute masterpiece of architecture, it is easily the largest secular space in Prague. It can be used for banquets, coronations, and other important events of the court, and its unprecedented size, not only in its length but also in the height of its vaulted ceiling, makes it even large enough to hold tournaments with knights on horseback inside it.*

---

*Historical info:*

*The Vladislav Hall, a late Gothic masterpiece in Prague Castle, was completed in 1502 under King Vladislav II. Designed by Benedikt Rejt, its vast space and ribbed vaults reflect medieval architectural ingenuity. Originally used for royal ceremonies, feasts, and jousting tournaments, it later hosted parliamentary meetings and royal elections during the Habsburg era. Its broad stairway was built to accommodate knights on horseback.*
