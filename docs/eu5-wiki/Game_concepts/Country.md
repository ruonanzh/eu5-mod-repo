A Country is a political entity that can participate in [Diplomacy](#Diplomacy). It has a [Government](#Government), a [Primary Culture](#Primary_Culture), and a [Primary Religion](#Primary_Religion). It may control [Armies](#Armies) and [Navies](#Navies) to project its power.

Each Country is played either by a human player or by an AI.

## Capital

Each country has a capital, which is the location where the government resides. Getting the capital [Occupied](#Occupied) in a [War](/Warfare "Warfare") can be devastating.

The capital can be moved to any core location provided there are enough goods in the market at the new location. Moving the capital costs 20 [![Stability.png](/thumb.php?f=Stability.png&width=24)](/Stability "Stability") Stability and takes a minimum of 180[[2]](#cite_note-2) days, increased by the distance between the old capital and the new one. The [![Resource gold.png](/thumb.php?f=Resource_gold.png&width=24)](/Gold "Gold") Gold cost to move it depends on the goods prices in the market where the new capital is located. After the capital is moved, any buildings that required the previous location to be the capital will be destroyed. Moving the capital can be cancelled at any point.

SOPs / Society of People / Pop based countries are landless tribal Nations found in Americas, Oceania, Indo-Burma and Siberia.
The population of SOP is location and culture based, you can colonize a location of the SOP but still have SOP present in the same location. If you expel, change culture or religion in the location, those pops removed from SOP population count.

Antagonism generation affect SOPs as well, this is especially dangerous if you are near Aztec region as SOPs close north have like 30k to 50k armies. Stealing maps is also not recommended unless you are on a different continent.

**Becoming landed nations**

Apparently SOPs could become landed nations with enough advances. I've never seen this happening as the continent usually colonized long before that.

## Control

Each capital, including Vassals and Fiefdoms generate proximity as well as governors. By default, this is 100% in the capital location and 80% in a governor location which spreads out and is gradually reduced over land and over sea if you own a town with a port building.

The reduction is scaled by % Proximity Speed, flat modifiers and constructed buildings.

This is then modified by your Land vs. Naval societal value to arrive at a final control.

Proximity is then multiplied by 0.75 to form your base control which is then modified by buildings, population satisfaction and if it is a core or not. This is then applied to the produced value of the the location, and distributed to your estates in accordance to their power. This is rounded down.

**Example:**

You have a **town** with a **temple** in a location of your culture (**core**) that is 4 locations away from your capital. The population is missing goods, and is not happy giving them a satisfaction reduction in control of 13%. The proximity to your capital is 45%.

Your total control would then be 45\*0,75(Proximity) + 20(Core) + 5(Town) + 5(Temple) - 13(Low Pop satisfaction) = 50,75 -> **50 Control**

The wealth share that goes to your non-crown estates is then taxed in accordance to their tax level.

**Example:**

* The location, described above produces **100** ducats in value, with 50 control.
* Nobles have 50% wealth share, Clergy 20% and Peasants 30%.

**Result:**

The location produces **100** ducats, which is reduced to **50** by your control value.

Your Nobles have 50% wealth share each, which means that **25** ducats goes into their monthly income, Clergy 20% so **10** ducats and peasants 30% so **15** ducats (which they can construct buildings with and loan to you).

This is then taxed, depending on the individual tax level of your estates which is added to your monthly income. Let us assume that the nobility has 10% tax, clergy 0% and peasants 25%. (This is manually adjustable by each estate on the slider setting).

In this situation you would gain **8.75** ducats (0.1 \* 25 + 0 \* 10 + 0.3 \* 15)

## Maritime presence

Each country has a maritime presence, which reduces the proximity that is lost between sea zones when calculating control. Maritime presence in the coastal sea zones of a market is also an important source of trade advantage. While at peace, each port will gradually increase maritime presence, while a blockade will quickly reduce it. A navy of light ships in a sea zone will rapidly increase maritime presence.

Privateers can be created from 250 Sailors by countries that meet the requirements to hire privateers. Privateers will reduce the maritime presence of rebels and countries at war with. For other countries, privateers display as pirates.

## Rebels

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| ![Wiki letter w.png](https://central.paradoxwikis.com/images/6/6a/Wiki_letter_w.png) | Please help improve this article or section by [**expanding it**](https://eu5.paradoxwikis.com/index.php?title=Country&action=edit) with: difference between secession and civil war. |

If pops do not have sufficient satisfaction, they will create a rebellion or join a rebellion with the same objectives. If enough pops in a location join a rebellion, the location will join the rebellion too.

Every rebellion has progress that goes from 0% to 100%. Rebellions gain progress at a rate of **0.1%** every month, and the rate is increased if control is low in the locations where pops joined the rebellion. When a mayor rebellion reaches 100% progress, all locations that joined the rebellion will form a new country and start a war against their previous owner. Minor rebellions cause a debuff in the locations the rebellion is present in. There are five types of rebellions:
