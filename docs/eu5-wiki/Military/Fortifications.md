## Zone of control

*Note that all the information there has been worked out by experimentation and does not come from knowledge of the actual game code, and thus might potentially be inaccurate.*

### Types of locations

As far as the zone of control (ZoC) mechanics are concerned there a three types of locations: Fort, ZoC and Empty. Most Fort locations generate a ZoC in all adjacent [locations](/Locations "Locations"). A ZoC cannot be generated across borders, even if they are with vassals or allies. This rule applies regardless of who controls the fort. Unlike EU4 the game tracks ZoCs per fort rather than treating all ZoC locations the same. Therefore a location may belong to the ZoC of multiple forts which is important for the movement rules.

### Movement Rules

#### Rule 1 - Empty locations

A unit may move from an empty location to any other location. It does not matter who owns the locations

#### Rule 2 - Ships

A unit may move from any location onto a sea tile, and conversely may move from a sea tile onto any location.

#### Rule 3 - Return

A unit may return to the location from which it entered its current location.

#### Rule 4 - Friendly locations

A unit may move into a friendly-controlled location from any location.

#### Rule 5 - Zone of control

A unit may move from one enemy ZoC into another as long as both zones are generated by the same combination of forts.

Example: Fort A creates ZoC A, within which a unit may move freely. Adding fort B creates ZoC B and an overlapping ZoC AB. A unit cannot move between ZoC A, ZoC B, and ZoC AB. Therefore, if a unit enters ZoC A from an empty province, it cannot reach fort B.

#### Rule 6 - Forts

A unit may move from an enemy ZoC onto any of the enemy forts that generate that zone of control.
